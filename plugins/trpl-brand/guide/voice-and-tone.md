# Voice and tone

**A knowledgeable park ranger who is excited to share what they know, not a
professor behind a podium.** Friendly, welcoming, plainspoken. Intellectually
serious without being inaccessible. Warm tone, serious mission.

The Library's voice does not imitate Roosevelt's. It aims to make people feel
what he made audiences feel: energized, included and ready to act. Before
writing, ask "How do I want the reader to feel?" rather than "What do I want to
say?"

## The voice is

Action-oriented · decisive · open-minded · heartfelt · good-humored · humble ·
optimistic · resilient · accessible · inspiring.

## Principles

- **Lead with story, not institution.** T.R.'s life is dramatic. Start with the
  person, the moment or the place, then give the institutional detail.
- **Be concrete.** Specific, checkable detail that lets people picture
  themselves here. Not "world-class experience" or "transformative journey."
- **Embrace the Badlands.** The landscape, the remoteness and the sense of
  adventure are the Library's greatest storytelling assets.
- **Connect past to present** honestly, through shared values, never through
  current political debates.
- **Respect the weight of the institution.** Conversational, never flippant.
- **Lead with strength.** Never open with a negative. State the good news, then
  acknowledge timing or limits.
- **Keep "we," "our," "I" and "us" to a minimum.** Write about what happened and
  what is there, not about the institution's journey.
- **Shorter is better.** Cut anything that does not earn its place.
- **Make the point. Don't announce it.** Cut throat-clearing: "it is worth
  noting," "it bears mentioning," "to put it plainly," "simply put."

## Practical tips

- Use precise language, the active voice and short sentences.
- Avoid jargon and museum-speak.
- Use inclusive language, accessible to every education level.
- Use stories to make things memorable.
- Fact-check everything. See [Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md).
- Stay nonpartisan.
- Measure what matters: action consistent with Leadership, Citizenship and
  Conservation, not clicks alone.

## Tone by audience

Stay inside the voice; adjust depth and formality.

| Audience | Adjust toward |
|----------|---------------|
| Families and travelers | Practical, inviting, specific about what to do and how long it takes |
| Educators and students | Clear, structured, curiosity-driven; age-appropriate |
| Benefactors | Composed and personal; gratitude stated plainly and once (see [Gratitude](editorial-style-guide.md#gratitude)) |
| Partners and sponsors | Confident and concise; what the partnership makes possible |
| Media | Factual, quotable, lead with the news |
| Officials | Formal, nonpartisan, shared values |
| Scholars | Precise, sourced, open about uncertainty and debate |
| Executive and institutional letters | Composed and formal, never folksy or familiar |

## Before and after

**Institution-first → story-first**

> ~~The Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library is proud to offer a world-class
> experience celebrating the legacy of our 26th president.~~
>
> In 1883 a young New Yorker stepped off the train in the Dakota Badlands to
> hunt bison. The Library stands in the same country, and tells what happened
> next.

*Every historical detail in an example like this still goes through the accuracy
check before it is published.*

**Throat-clearing → the point**

> ~~It is worth noting that tickets are timed.~~
>
> Tickets are timed. Choose an entry time when you book.

**Leading with a negative → leading with strength**

> ~~Unfortunately, the Library is a long drive from most airports.~~
>
> The drive is part of the trip: the Library sits in the Badlands, an easy stop
> on the way to Theodore Roosevelt National Park.
