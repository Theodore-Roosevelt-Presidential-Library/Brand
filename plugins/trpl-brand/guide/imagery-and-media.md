# Imagery and media

## Two lanes

- **Contemporary imagery is nature and place:** the Badlands, the Little
  Missouri River, big sky, prairie, wildlife, and the Library itself.
- **Historical imagery is Roosevelt:** his life, his family and his world.

Don't mix the lanes carelessly. When you layer old and new (which the brand
encourages), make the juxtaposition deliberate.

## Photography rules

- **Real photographs only** for the Library, its buildings and its people.
  **Never** use the architectural renderings; the building is open.
- **Start with the Library's own library.** Images in the Library's digital
  asset library (Acquia DAM) are cleared for Library use. Staff and approved
  partners request access through Marketing & Communications.
- **Otherwise use public-domain or properly licensed images**, credited as the
  source asks, and only with a catalog record you can point to (see below).
- **Text over photos:** for legibility, place a Night Sky gradient (solid to
  transparent) behind the text, as in the Library's presentation template.
- When a photo is the main visual, it acts as the background color. Add one
  grounding **or** one accent color, not both. See [Visual identity](visual-identity.md#principles).

## AI-generated imagery

**Allowed only for landscape and wildlife:** Badlands, buttes, prairie, bison,
wild horses, elk, rivers, canyons. Even then, prefer a real photograph.

**Never:**

- People, including T.R., his family or anyone historical.
- The Library, the North Dakota State Capitol, Mount Rushmore, Devils Tower,
  ranch buildings, or **any** identifiable building or structure. Image models
  invent architecture with confidence and get it wrong. Add "no buildings, no
  architecture, no monuments, no structures of any kind" to every prompt.
- Anything presented as a historical photograph or document.
- Illustrations that change the historical character of what they depict.

## Historical images and film of Theodore Roosevelt: where to look

Start with the holding institution. Every caption fact (date, place, people,
photographer) must match the institution's catalog record. A Wikipedia or
Pinterest copy is not a record; use a reverse image search to find the original.

### Film rules

- Use the highest-resolution copy available.
- **Remove every archive-made title card**, including archive re-creations of
  original titles and cards added at later restoration. Keep only what is
  original to the film.
- Reels that hold several unrelated events are split into separate clips
  (A, B, C in original reel order). Each clip still cites the parent reel.
- Material from the Theodore Roosevelt Association is credited to the TRA.
- Footage from the Spanish-American War era includes staged and re-enacted
  scenes. Check what a film actually shows before captioning it.

### Identifying who and what is in an image

Treat every identification as a claim. Ask HistorianPanel to check the image
and caption before publishing ("Is this caption right?"). It has specialists for
photograph identification, film and cartoons, and period-appropriate visuals.
See [Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md).

## Credits

- Credit the holding institution as its rights statement asks
  (for example "Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division").
- Press photos in social posts carry a credit line: `Photos: Name`.
- Never crop a credit or watermark out of an image you do not have rights to.
