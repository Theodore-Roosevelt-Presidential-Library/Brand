# TRPL Voice & Tone

## The Core Voice

TRPL's voice is **action-oriented, decisive, inspiring, and earnest.**

The best mental model: a knowledgeable park ranger who's genuinely excited to share what they know. Not a professor behind a podium. Not a marketing copywriter. Not a tour guide reading from a script. Someone who has actually been to these places, knows these stories, and wants you to feel what they feel.

We are intellectually serious without being inaccessible. Warm without being breezy. Authoritative without being stuffy.

## What We Are / What We're Not

| We Are | We Are Not |
|---|---|
| Story-forward | Institution-forward |
| Specific and concrete | Vague and aspirational |
| Earnest | Ironic or clever |
| Inviting | Exclusive or academic |
| Grounded in the Badlands | Generic "world-class experience" language |
| Inspiring civic engagement | Partisan or political |
| Respectful of the weight of TR's legacy | Reverential to the point of being cold |

## Writing Principles

### Lead with story, not institution
TR's life is inherently dramatic. Open with a specific moment, a vivid detail, a surprising fact — before you say anything about the Library. The story earns the institutional message.

**Weak:** "The Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library is excited to announce our upcoming grand opening on July 4, 2026."

**Strong:** "When Theodore Roosevelt first arrived in the Badlands in 1883, he came to hunt. He stayed — and was remade. On July 4, 2026, the Library he inspired opens in the landscape that changed him."

### Be concrete, not generic
Avoid aspirational filler. "World-class experience," "transformative journey," "immersive storytelling" — these phrases have been used so often they communicate nothing. Replace them with specifics that let people picture themselves there.

**Weak:** "A world-class, immersive experience awaits."

**Strong:** "Stand in the room where TR wrote. Walk the trail where he rode. Hear his voice — reconstructed from recordings made in his own lifetime."

### Connect past to present — carefully
The Library exists not just to preserve history but to inspire civic engagement today. Draw authentic lines between TR's era and the present. But do so carefully — without forcing it, without partisanship, without claiming TR as the property of any political side.

### Use TR's words precisely
When quoting TR, use his exact words with source attribution. Never paraphrase as a quote. If you don't have a verified source, describe the idea he expressed rather than invent or approximate a quote.

## Tone by Channel

### Social Media (Long-form: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn)
- Personal, story-driven, behind-the-scenes feel
- Longer captions that build to a point
- End with trlibrary.com/visit and relevant hashtags
- Tag partners and guests with verified handles

### Social Media (Short-form: X, Threads, Bluesky)
- Punchy, single-idea posts
- Hooks first — no preamble
- Same CTA discipline: drive to visit link

### Email
- More formal than social but warmer than press
- Subject lines: specific and curiosity-driven, not click-bait
- Plain language; no museum-speak

### Press Releases / Media Materials
- Objective tone, AP style guidance from CMOS 17th ed.
- Facts first, quotes second, context third
- Ed O'Keefe for national/major media quotes; Robbie Lauf for regional/North Dakota-specific angles

### Exhibit Copy / In-Building Text
- Designed to be read on feet, in motion
- Short sentences. Active voice. Present tense where possible.
- Every panel should leave the participant wanting to know what happens next.

### Donor / Benefactor Communications
- Formal but relationship-driven
- Values giving insiders early, candid information consistent with their status
- Use "benefactors" not "donors"; never "board" — always "Trustees"

## Terminology Reference

| Use | Never Use |
|---|---|
| participants | visitors, guests |
| Trustees | board members |
| benefactors | donors |
| the Library (capitalized) | the library |
| the Foundation (capitalized) | the foundation |
| T.R. (with periods) | TR (no periods) in formal prose |
| Medora | The Badlands (as a substitute for Medora) |

**"Teddy"** is never used in prose — the one exception is the hashtag #TeddyRoosevelt for social search discovery.
