# Brand foundation

Source: Brand Style Guide v0.1, Part I ([trlibrary.com/brand](https://www.trlibrary.com/brand)).

## Brand story

The Library is rooted in a place, a person and a purpose. In Medora, the
landscape that shaped Roosevelt becomes the setting for a new kind of
presidential library, one that looks to the past to help people face the future.

It is a **people's library**. Roosevelt is not the destination; he is a guide.
It is a place to learn **with and from** T.R., not only **about** him.

## Mission

> Explore TR's life, legacy, and enduring relevance.

His life becomes a lens for exploring leadership, citizenship and conservation,
and for asking what it means to live with purpose.

*The mission is quoted as written in the Brand Style Guide. In running copy,
apply house style: T.R. with periods.*

## Vision

> Inspire action and fearless participation in the arena.

Move people from reflection to action: off the sidelines and into the arena.

## Values

**Dare Greatly. Think Boldly. Live Passionately. Care Deeply.**

- **Dare Greatly:** the courage to try.
- **Think Boldly:** looking beyond the immediate, imagining what is possible.
- **Live Passionately:** embracing life with energy and purpose.
- **Care Deeply:** taking responsibility for people, places and ideas beyond ourselves.

These are the Library's official Values. **They are not T.R. quotations.** Never
put them in quotation marks or attribute them to him.

## Pillars

- **Leadership:** not holding authority, but stepping forward and taking
  responsibility. The Library does not prescribe a model of leadership; it helps
  participants find their own.
- **Citizenship:** a responsibility, not a title, practiced every day.
- **Conservation:** Roosevelt's conservation legacy, carried forward through
  prairie restoration, sustainable systems and a building designed to work with
  its environment.

Conservation figures (acres, numbers of forests, refuges, monuments) are
historical claims. Source each one before publishing; see
[Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md).

## Ten storytelling tenets

Not every tenet applies to every story, but consider all of them.

1. **Inspire action in the arena.** Equip people to learn, care, think and act.
   The end of a visit is the beginning of their journey.
2. **The participant is the hero.** Roosevelt is the guide.
3. **Stories come to life.** Beyond "show, don't tell" to "do, don't tell."
4. **Nature is fundamental.** Draw people inside to encourage them to go outside.
5. **Engage head, hands and heart.** Learning, action, and love and loss.
6. **The people's library.** Home to many stories, not one.
7. **Lessons are for today and tomorrow.** Engage the positive and the problematic.
8. **Worthy of its presidential stature.** Engaging, never an amusement park.
9. **Celebrate the old, embrace the new.** New technology alongside analog craft.
10. **Humanize, don't lionize.** Face the contradictions directly. There is no
    "their side" or "our side."

## Audiences

Global and intergenerational, with the local community at the heart of the work.
The Library aims to be part of the fabric of Medora and to support local
businesses and partners.

Key audiences: families and travelers planning trips to Medora; educators and
students; Benefactors and prospective Benefactors; corporate and nonprofit
partners; media; government officials; and the Roosevelt scholarly community.

**"Participant" is intentional.** It describes an active role, not a passive
audience. See the [editorial style guide](editorial-style-guide.md#participants).
