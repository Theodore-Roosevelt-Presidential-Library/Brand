# Accuracy and approvals

## The accuracy rule

**Nothing historical carries the Library's name until every claim in it has two
independent sources.** As a presidential library, the Library's credibility
depends on getting the facts right, and a misattributed quote is the most likely
way to embarrass it in public.

This applies to everyone (staff, agencies, partners and AI tools) and to every
channel: web pages, captions and alt text, social posts, newsletters, press
releases, decks, scripts and narration, exhibit text, quizzes, grant and donor
materials, merchandise and datasets.

**A claim** is anything a reader could check: a quote; a date, number, name or
place; an office held; a sequence ("before," "succeeded"); a superlative
("first," "only"); who is in a picture, and when and where it was taken; an
anecdote told as fact.

### What counts as a source

- At least one should be **primary** (the T.R. Papers, Library of Congress,
  Houghton Library, NPS, the Congressional Record, period newspapers) or an
  **authoritative reference** (the Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress,
  White House or National Archives lists, the Theodore Roosevelt Center, a
  standard scholarly biography).
- The two sources must be **independent**: two websites repeating the same
  article count as one.

### What never counts as a source

- Any AI output, including Claude, ChatGPT or the Library's own AI assistants
- trlibrary.com itself, and tools the Library built
- The Story Guide (it governs tone, not fact)
- Wikipedia on its own

### Quotes and images

- Quotes must match a verified source word for word. Check the
  [misquotes](https://www.trlibrary.com/tr/quotes/misquotes) and
  [Did TR Really Say That?](https://www.trlibrary.com/tr/fake-quotes) pages first.
- Image captions must match the holding institution's catalog record. No record
  means no specific date or list of people in the caption.
- When reputable sources disagree, say so, or use the narrower claim. Never pick
  one silently.

## How to check: HistorianPanel

[HistorianPanel](https://github.com/Theodore-Roosevelt-Presidential-Library/HistorianPanel)
is the Library's panel of AI accuracy specialists. It breaks copy into claims,
checks each against two independent sources, re-checks blind, and marks each
claim keep, fix, hedge or cut, with sources. It finds sources; it is never a
source.

Installing this brand kit offers to install HistorianPanel too. Then ask, in
plain words: "Is this caption right?", "Check this page for accuracy," "When did
T.R. first come to Dakota?"

**Accuracy and brand are separate checks.** HistorianPanel checks whether
something is true. This kit checks whether it sounds, reads and looks like the
Library. Most pieces need both.

## What needs review

All external work for the Library goes through **Marketing & Communications**,
which routes it to the right reviewers. Allow extra time when a piece:

| Contains | Reviewed by |
|----------|-------------|
| Historical claims, quotes, artifact or image identifications | Curatorial |
| References to a sitting or former President, or presidential visits | Leadership and counsel |
| Elected officials, policy or anything politically adjacent | Leadership and external communications counsel |
| Sustainability or Living Building Challenge performance figures | The Library's sustainability lead and consultants |
| Financial figures, gift amounts, Benefactor names | Development |
| Indigenous history, people, places or nations, including the MHA Nation | The Library's designated reviewers, with tribal partners |
| Attendance or visitation figures | Marketing & Communications (use total tickets sold, never partial counts) |

Specific figures belong in conversation more than in documents that can
circulate. When in doubt, leave a number out and ask.
