# Editorial style guide

The rules most often needed when writing for the Library. Entries are A to Z.
Each entry gives the rule, then the reason or an example where it helps.

**Rule levels.** **Always** and **Never** are fixed rules. **Prefer** is the
default, and you can depart from it with a reason. Anything not covered here:
follow the [voice](voice-and-tone.md), flag the question to Marketing &
Communications, and propose a rule (see [How this guide works](README.md#keeping-the-guide-current)).

## Quick reference

| Use | Not |
|-----|-----|
| participants | visitors, guests, audience |
| Trustees | board members |
| Benefactors | donors |
| the Library, the Foundation | the library, the foundation |
| T.R. | TR, Teddy |
| buttes | mesas |
| Kettle Hill; the San Juan Heights | San Juan Hill |
| the Boardwalk | the Cultural Loop |
| independently operated | privately funded |
| pursuing (a certification) | certified, achieved (until confirmed) |
| a specific detail | world-class, transformative, unforgettable experience |

## A

### Accessibility

**Always** write alt text for every meaningful image. Describe what is shown and
why it matters in context. For historical photographs, the alt text follows the
same accuracy rules as the caption: no date, place or sitter that the holding
institution's record does not support. Captions and transcripts accompany video
and audio.

### AI-generated images

**Never** use AI-generated images of people, of the Library, or of any real,
identifiable building or landmark. See [Imagery and media](imagery-and-media.md#ai-generated-imagery).
**Never** present an AI image as a photograph.

### Ampersand (&)

**Prefer** "and" in running text. Use "&" only in proper names that use it (for
example, a named gallery or partner) and in tight design layouts.

### Attribution

**Always** attribute quotations and historical images. See [Quotations](#quotations)
and [Credits and captions](#credits-and-captions).

## B

### Benefactors

**Always** "Benefactors," capitalized, for people and organizations that give
to the Library. **Never** "donors" in public copy.

> Benefactors made the Library possible.

### The Boardwalk

**Always** "the Boardwalk" for the elevated walkway. **Never** "the Cultural
Loop," the name used in early design documents.

### Buttes

**Always** "buttes" for North Dakota landforms. **Never** "mesas." Proper nouns
keep their names (Mesa Verde).

## C

### Capitalization

- **Always** capitalize **the Library** and **the Foundation** when they refer
  to this institution.
- **Always** capitalize **Trustees** and **Benefactors**.
- **Always** capitalize the four Values when used as the Values: Dare Greatly,
  Think Boldly, Live Passionately, Care Deeply.

### Certifications and sustainability claims

**Always** "pursuing" Living Building Challenge certification (or any other
certification) until certification is formally confirmed. **Never**
"certified," "achieved" or "net-zero building" as a completed fact.
Performance figures (energy, water, carbon) need approval before publishing;
see [Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md#what-needs-review).

### Counting

**Never** count items when introducing links or lists ("Four pages follow
Alice..."). Give each link a title and a short teaser that invites the reader to
go deeper.

### Credits and captions

- Historical images: caption facts (date, place, people, photographer) must
  match the holding institution's catalog record. Credit the institution as it
  asks to be credited.
- Press photos in social posts: add a credit line (`Photos: Name`).
- Material from the Theodore Roosevelt Association (TRA) is credited to the TRA.
- Captions use Frutiger Next in design (see [Visual identity](visual-identity.md#typography)).

## D

### Dates and seasons

- **Never** put the current or a future year in evergreen visitor copy ("the
  2027 season"). It goes stale. Write seasons as months ("May through
  September").
- Historical dates: month day, year (October 27, 1858). Verify every date.

## E

### Email

- **Always** use "Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library" as the From name, for
  every email and automation. Not "...Foundation."
- Subject lines: plain and specific. Say what is inside.

### "Experience"

**Never** use "experience" as aspirational filler ("a world-class experience,"
"an unforgettable experience"). Say what people do, see or learn.

## F

### Filler and superlatives

**Never** use unverifiable superlatives (world-class, best, unmatched,
transformative journey). Historical "firsts," "onlys," "youngests" and
"largests" are factual claims: each needs a source, or it is cut.

### First reference

**Prefer** "Theodore Roosevelt" on first reference, then "T.R." or "Roosevelt."
For the period-correct title (Governor, President, Colonel), see
[Names and titles for T.R.](#names-and-titles-for-tr).

### Funding

**Always** "independently operated." **Never** "privately funded."

## G

### Gratitude

Thanks to Benefactors, partners, volunteers and staff should be **plain,
specific and said once.** Gratitude spread through every sentence reads as
insincere, and stacked adjectives say less than one specific fact.

**Always**

- Thank once, near the top or at the close, not in every paragraph.
- Name what the person or organization did and what it makes possible. The
  specific outcome carries the thanks.
- Use the name the Benefactor asked to be recognized by. Confirm it with
  Development before publishing.
- Keep executive and institutional letters composed and formal.

**Never**

- Stack intensifiers: "deeply and sincerely grateful for your incredibly
  generous support."
- Repeat "thank you" in several forms in one short piece.
- Publish a gift amount or donor figure without Development's approval.
- Use folksy or familiar language in letters from leadership.

**Before**

> We are so incredibly grateful and truly humbled by your amazingly generous
> gift. Thank you, thank you, thank you! Your support means the world to us, and
> we couldn't be more thankful to have you on this journey with us.

**After**

> Thank you for your gift to the Library's education fund. It brings school
> groups from across western North Dakota to Medora this spring, many for their
> first visit to a presidential library.

*(Use the real program and outcome. Never invent an impact.)*

**In social posts and public acknowledgments:** one line of thanks, tag the
partner where the platform allows, then the substance.

**In press releases:** thank sponsors and Benefactors in a single sentence or a
standard closing paragraph, not throughout.

## H

### Hashtags

- **#DareGreatly** is evergreen. **#TR250** is the campaign hashtag.
- **#TeddyRoosevelt** is allowed as a hashtag for search discovery. It is the
  only place "Teddy" may appear.
- Verify every handle before tagging.

### Hours

**Never** restate operating hours in other copy. Link to
[trlibrary.com/visit/hours](https://www.trlibrary.com/visit/hours), the one
place hours live. The Library is open **year-round with seasonal hours**;
never describe it as summer-only.

### Hyphenation

**Never** use automatic hyphenation to break words at line ends, in any layout.
Adjust line breaks, tracking or copy instead.

## I

### Indigenous peoples and nations

Use the name each nation uses for itself. Content about Indigenous history,
people or places, including the Mandan, Hidatsa and Arikara (MHA) Nation, is
always reviewed before publishing. See [Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md#what-needs-review).

## K

### Kettle Hill and the San Juan Heights

**Always** "Kettle Hill" for the Rough Riders' charge and "the San Juan Heights"
for the wider battle. **Never** "San Juan Hill" in prose. It may appear only
inside a proper name or a published title.

## L

### Library, Foundation

- **The Library:** the public-facing name of the institution and the place.
  Use it in nearly all copy.
- **The Foundation:** the Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library Foundation,
  the organization. Use it for legal, financial and governance contexts.
- The Library is **not** part of the National Archives (NARA) presidential
  library system. Roosevelt's presidency predates that system. Describe this
  neutrally. **Never** say the Library "bypassed" NARA or that NARA
  "constrains" anyone.

### Links

- Link text says where the link goes ("Plan your visit"), never "click here."
- Every public post or email includes a way to visit: trlibrary.com/visit or a
  ticket link. Press-coverage posts are the exception; see [Channels](channels.md#social-media).

## M

### Mesa

See [Buttes](#buttes).

## N

### Names and titles for T.R.

- **Always** "T.R." with periods in prose.
- **Never** "Teddy" in prose, headlines, captions or alt text. Hashtag exception: #TeddyRoosevelt. The common noun "teddy bear"
  is fine when the subject is the toy.
- Use the title correct for the period discussed: **Governor Roosevelt** for his
  governorship of New York, **President Roosevelt** for his presidency, and
  **Colonel Roosevelt** for the years after the presidency (the Story Guide's
  convention). "Theodore" and "T.R." work in any period.

### Nonpartisan

**Always** nonpartisan. Roosevelt's legacy belongs to no party. Ground
contemporary connections in shared values, not current political debates.
**Never** name recent or current political figures in Library copy without
review. See [Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md#what-needs-review).

### Numbers

Specific figures (attendance, finances, gifts, sustainability performance) need
approval before they appear in writing. See
[Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md#what-needs-review).

## P

### Participants

**Always** "participants" for people who visit or take part in the Library's
programs. **Never** "visitors," "guests" or "audience" in Library copy.

**Why.** The brand foundation casts people in an active role. Participants step
into the arena; visitors watch from the sidelines.

**Exceptions.** Proper names keep their wording (a park's "Visitor Center"), and
quotations are never changed.

> ~~Guests can explore three galleries.~~
>
> Participants explore three galleries.

The rule is about the noun for people. "Visit" as a verb or a call to action
("Plan your visit," "Visit the Library") is fine.

Where "participants" feels stiff, rewrite around the person: "you," "families,"
"students," "everyone who comes through the doors."

### Present tense

The Library is open. **Never** use pre-opening or future-tense language about
the Library itself ("will open," "will feature").

## Q

### Quotations

T.R.'s words are quoted **verbatim. Always.**

- **Never** paraphrase, modernize, shorten without ellipses, or reconstruct a
  Roosevelt quotation.
- **Always** cite the source (speech, letter or book, with the date). In design,
  historic T.R. quotes carry their location and year.
- **Always** check [Misquotes](https://www.trlibrary.com/tr/quotes/misquotes) and
  [Did TR Really Say That?](https://www.trlibrary.com/tr/fake-quotes) before
  using any quotation.
- If the wording cannot be verified, describe the idea without quotation
  marks, or cut it.
- **Never** use: *"I never would have been President if it had not been for my
  experiences in North Dakota"* or its variants. It is widely repeated, and
  its exact wording has not been verified.
- The four Values are not quotations. See [Brand foundation](brand-foundation.md#values).

Every quotation goes through the accuracy check. See
[Accuracy and approvals](accuracy-and-approvals.md).

## R

### Renderings

**Never** use the Library's architectural renderings. The building is open: use
real photographs.

## S

### San Juan Hill

See [Kettle Hill and the San Juan Heights](#kettle-hill-and-the-san-juan-heights).

### Signatures

The Teddy Regular typeface (from T.R.'s handwriting) is **never** used as, or
presented as, his signature. See [Visual identity](visual-identity.md#typography).

## T

### T.R.

See [Names and titles for T.R.](#names-and-titles-for-tr).

### Throat-clearing

**Never** announce what is about to be said. Cut "it is worth noting," "it
bears mentioning," "to put it plainly," "simply put," "let's be clear." Lead with
the fact.

### Trustees

**Always** "Trustees," capitalized. **Never** "board members."

## V

### Values

See [Brand foundation](brand-foundation.md#values). Not T.R. quotations.

### Visit length

A standard visit is **about three and a half hours**. **Never** suggest a
shorter visit is typical or enough, in copy, itineraries or tools.

## W

### We, our, us

**Prefer** minimal first person in institutional copy. Write about the place,
the story and the participant.
